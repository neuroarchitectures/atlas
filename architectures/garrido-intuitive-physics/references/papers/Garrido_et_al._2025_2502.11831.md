# Intuitive physics understanding emerges from self-supervised pretraining on natural videos

**Authors:** Luis Garrido et al. (Meta FAIR / NYU)
**arXiv:** 2502.11831 (2025)

## Abstract

We investigate whether self-supervised video representations truly understand the physical world or merely memorize visual patterns. Using a violation-of-expectation framework — if a model exhibits higher "surprise" (prediction error) for physically impossible events, it understands the corresponding physical law — we test three intuitive physics properties: object permanence, shape consistency, and spatial continuity. Our key finding: latent-space prediction models (V-JEPA) show above-chance performance on multiple intuitive physics tests, while pixel-space prediction models and text-reasoning multimodal LLMs perform near chance. Even models trained on just one week of unique video show above-chance performance. This demonstrates that jointly learning abstract representations and predicting missing sensory input (predictive coding) is sufficient for intuitive physics understanding, challenging the assumption that core knowledge requires hardcoding.
